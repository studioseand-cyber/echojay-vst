CORRECTION, 7 Oct 20:0x, after reading the crash report (.ips BC815958, 19:12:25). My attribution above - "a network
callback writes EchoJayAPI members from a network thread, aliveToken_ not consulted" - IS WRONG. Those members are
CLASS STATICS (EchoJayAPI.h:1085-1097) and outlive every instance, and the faulting thread is not ours: it is
com.apple.NSURLSession-work inside -[__NSCFURLSessionDelegateWrapper didBecomeInvalidWithError:] ->
-[NSURLSession finalizeDelegateWithError:], SIGSEGV at 0x0. CFNetwork is finalising the shared NSURLSession and
messaging a delegate that has already been released - JUCE's JUCE_URLDelegate_, owned by SharedSession
(juce_Network_mac.mm:132-200) and destroyed when the last in-flight request goes away. MallocScribble is why only
the scribble leg sees it: the released delegate's memory is poisoned rather than merely stale.
What IS ours, and is a real defect: ~EchoJayProcessor joins its own threads (scanner, loadThread, saveThread) and
then exits with up to seven detached NETWORK workers still inside a blocking read, the config fetch's connection
timeout being 60 seconds. Quitting a host within a minute of opening EchoJay, offline or on a slow network, is the
condition in that report. Measured rate before any fix: 0 of 20 runs red unsealed, 0 of 20 through run_guard.sh
--scribble as ctest runs it, with a request still in flight at exit in 15 of 20 - one crash in about 41 runs. The
fix and the leg are written up as 06d item 1 in HANDOFF_COMP_PROFILES_PLUGIN.md. NOTHING IN THE 06c BINARIES
CHANGES because of this correction: the defect is older than this round and was only made visible by it.
